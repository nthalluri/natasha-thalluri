## Fieldwork on Georgian 

Since 2022, I have conducted fieldwork on Georgian on a variety of topics as part of the WOLF-Lab at Harvard. With the support of the Georgian Program at the Davis Center of Eurasian Studies at Harvard, I have been on multiple field trips to Tbilisi, Georgia. 

- (Contra) factivity- It has been argued that natural language lacks true contrafactive, i.e predicates that presuppose the falsity of their complements. While it is certainly typologically rare, there are some crosslinguistic examples of attitude predicates that seem to be contrafactive. I investigate one such contrafactive predicate in Georgian.
- Clausal complementation- I helped to develop a database of clausal complementation and argument structure in Georgian as part of the WOLF-Lab.

## Type Economy

For my MA thesis, I investigated an extension of Heim’s theory of Type Economy which has primarily been applied to the case of conjunction where it has been shown that all type lifted variants of and are derivable from the sentential and. When we extend this argument to the domain of comparatives, a similar case can been made for the comparative morpheme -er with the 3-place phrasal comparative being derivable in terms of the 2-place clausal one. While it is possible to account for the English comparative cases by assuming a simpler semantics of -er and a covert clausal syntax, I show that the cross-linguistic extension of this logic fails with evidence from Hindi comparatives.
